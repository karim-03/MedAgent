# Heart Disease Risk Factors

## Overview

Several health conditions, lifestyle, and age and family history can
increase risk for heart disease. These are called risk factors. Key risk
factors for heart disease include high blood pressure, high cholesterol,
and smoking. Some risk factors cannot be controlled, such as age or family
history, but steps can be taken to lower risk by changing the factors that
can be controlled.

## High blood pressure

High blood pressure is a major risk factor for heart disease. It happens
when the pressure of the blood in the arteries and other blood vessels is
too high. If not controlled, it can affect the heart and other major
organs, including the kidneys and brain. High blood pressure is often
called a "silent killer" because it usually has no symptoms — the only way
to know is to measure it. It can be lowered with lifestyle changes or
medicine.

## Unhealthy blood cholesterol levels

Cholesterol is a waxy, fat-like substance made by the liver or found in
certain foods. If more cholesterol is taken in than the body can use, the
extra cholesterol can build up in the walls of the arteries, including
those of the heart, leading to narrowing of the arteries and decreased
blood flow to the heart, brain, kidneys, and other parts of the body.

There are two main types of blood cholesterol:
- LDL (low-density lipoprotein) cholesterol — considered "bad" cholesterol
  because it can cause plaque buildup in the arteries.
- HDL (high-density lipoprotein) cholesterol — considered "good"
  cholesterol because higher levels provide some protection against heart
  disease.

High blood cholesterol usually has no signs or symptoms — the only way to
know is a blood test called a "lipid profile."

## Diabetes mellitus

The body needs glucose (sugar) for energy, and insulin (made in the
pancreas) helps move glucose from food into the body's cells. In diabetes,
the body doesn't make enough insulin, can't use its own insulin as well as
it should, or both — causing sugar to build up in the blood. The risk of
death from heart disease for adults with diabetes is higher than for
adults who do not have diabetes.

## Obesity

Obesity is excess body fat. It is linked to higher "bad" cholesterol and
triglyceride levels and lower "good" cholesterol levels, and can lead to
high blood pressure and diabetes as well as heart disease.

## Behaviors that increase risk

Eating a diet high in saturated fats, trans fat, and cholesterol has been
linked to heart disease and related conditions such as atherosclerosis.
Too much salt (sodium) in the diet can raise blood pressure. Not getting
enough physical activity can lead to heart disease and increase the
chances of other risk factors (obesity, high blood pressure, high
cholesterol, diabetes). Drinking too much alcohol can raise blood pressure
and triglyceride levels. Tobacco use increases the risk for heart disease
and heart attack — nicotine raises blood pressure, and carbon monoxide
from cigarette smoke reduces the amount of oxygen the blood can carry.

## Genetics, age, sex, race and ethnicity

Genetic factors likely play some role in high blood pressure, heart
disease, and related conditions, though people with a family history of
heart disease also often share common environments and other risk factors.
Risk increases even more when heredity combines with unhealthy lifestyle
choices. Heart disease is the number one killer of both men and women, can
happen at any age, and risk goes up with age. Heart disease is the leading
cause of death for people of most racial and ethnic groups in the United
States, including African Americans, American Indians and Alaska Natives,
and White people; for Asian American, Pacific Islander, and Hispanic
people, it is second only to cancer.
