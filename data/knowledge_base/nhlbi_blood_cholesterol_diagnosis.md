# Blood Cholesterol — Diagnosis

A healthcare provider diagnoses high blood cholesterol based on medical and
family history, a physical exam, and a blood test of cholesterol levels.

## Medical history and physical exam

A provider will ask about eating habits, physical activity, family
history, medicines, and other risk factors for heart or blood vessel
disease. During the physical exam, the provider will check for signs of
very high blood cholesterol, such as xanthomas (fatty deposits under the
skin), or signs of other conditions that can cause high cholesterol.

## Screening — the lipid panel

A provider may order a blood test called a lipid panel (also called a
lipoprotein panel or lipid profile) to screen for unhealthy cholesterol
levels. Fasting for 8 to 12 hours beforehand may be required. A lipid
panel measures:

- **Total cholesterol**
- **LDL ("bad") cholesterol** — the main source of cholesterol buildup and
  blockages in the arteries
- **HDL ("good") cholesterol** — helps decrease cholesterol blockages in
  the arteries
- **Triglycerides** — another type of fat in the blood

The goal for a healthy lipid profile is generally non-HDL cholesterol
below 130 mg/dL, with HDL of at least 40 mg/dL for men and 50 mg/dL for
women (individual targets vary — check with a provider).

### Recommended screening frequency

- **Age 19 or younger**: screening begins at ages 9–11, repeated every 5
  years (as early as age 2 if there is a family history of high
  cholesterol, heart attack, or stroke).
- **Ages 20–65**: younger adults screened every 5 years; men ages 45–65
  and women ages 55–65 screened every 1–2 years.
- **Older than 65**: screened every year.

## Lipoprotein-a (Lp(a))

An Lp(a) test is not usually part of a routine lipid panel. High Lp(a)
levels may indicate higher risk of heart or blood vessel disease even if
other cholesterol levels are healthy. Lp(a) level is largely determined by
inherited genes and is unlikely to change much from childhood to old age.
A provider may order this test if there is a family history of stroke or
early heart disease, or an unknown family medical history. If Lp(a) is
high, a provider may prescribe a statin even if other cholesterol levels
are in the healthy range.
