# High Blood Pressure — Diagnosis

## Screening

Everyone age 3 or older should have blood pressure checked by a healthcare
provider at least once a year. Regular checks can also show how well
treatment for high blood pressure is working. Preparation for an accurate
measurement includes: no exercise, coffee, or cigarettes for 30 minutes
before the test; using the bathroom beforehand; sitting relaxed for at
least 5 minutes with feet flat on the floor; not talking during the test;
and resting the arm at heart level. Readings can be inaccurate due to
"white coat hypertension" (higher readings in a provider's office than at
home), "masked high blood pressure" (lower readings at the office than
elsewhere), or an incorrectly sized cuff.

## What the numbers mean

Blood pressure is reported as two numbers: the top (systolic) number,
measured when the heart pumps and pressure is highest, and the bottom
(diastolic) number, measured between heartbeats when pressure is lowest —
for example, "120 over 80." For most adults, a healthy blood pressure is
less than 120/80 mm Hg. Research has shown that systolic blood pressure
higher than 120 mm Hg can cause harm to health over time, including organ
damage. Readings above 180/120 mm Hg are dangerously high and require
immediate medical attention.

## Blood pressure categories

| Category | Systolic (mm Hg) | | Diastolic (mm Hg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | Less than 120 | AND | Less than 80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 | AND | Less than 80 |
| High Blood Pressure Stage 1 | 130–139 | OR | 80–89 |
| High Blood Pressure Stage 2 | 140 or higher | OR | 90 or higher |
| Hypertensive Crisis | Higher than 180 | OR | Higher than 120 (seek immediate care) |

## Confirming a diagnosis

Two or more blood pressure readings at separate medical appointments are
generally needed to diagnose high blood pressure. A provider may also ask
about eating patterns, physical activity level, sleep, body weight,
stress, smoking status, and family health history to understand risk
factors and set up a treatment plan, and may order further tests to check
whether high blood pressure has already affected other organs.
