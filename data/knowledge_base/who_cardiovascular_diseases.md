## Global burden

Cardiovascular diseases are described by WHO as the leading cause of death
globally. WHO's cardiovascular disease work sits within its broader effort
against noncommunicable diseases, which together account for a large share
of deaths worldwide, and cardiovascular disease alone is estimated to
cause on the order of tens of millions of deaths annually. A large
majority of cardiovascular deaths are attributed to heart attacks and
strokes, and a substantial share occur prematurely, in people under 70.

## Behavioral and environmental risk factors

WHO identifies the most important behavioral risk factors for heart
disease and stroke as: unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, tobacco use,
and harmful use of alcohol. Air pollution is highlighted as a significant
environmental risk factor. These behavioral and environmental exposures
tend to show up in individuals as intermediate risk factors — raised blood
pressure, raised blood glucose, raised blood lipids, and overweight or
obesity — which can be measured in primary care settings and signal
increased risk of heart attack, stroke, heart failure, and other
complications.

## Prevention

WHO guidance emphasizes that stopping tobacco use, reducing dietary salt,
eating more fruit and vegetables, engaging in regular physical activity,
and avoiding harmful alcohol use have all been shown to reduce
cardiovascular disease risk. High blood pressure in particular is
highlighted by WHO as one of the largest single contributors to
cardiovascular deaths worldwide.
